The Glasgow Declaration on a Transport Data Commons
***************************************************

:slug: glasgow-declaration

During 23 and 24 February 2026,
many members of the Transport Data Commons Initative
and the global community of transport data stakeholders
gathered at University of Strathclyde in Glasgow, Scotland.

Through intense work sessions,
they discussed the need for and value of accessible,
high-quality transport data,
and drafted more than 100 *calls to action*
directed at the people and organizations who can act together
to realize a Transport Data Commons.

The most widely agreed and urgent actions are collected in the
**Glasgow Declaration on a Transport Data Commons**,
which has been signed by more than 50 organizations and individuals.

The final text of the Declaration and full list of signatories is available
`here <https://docs.google.com/document/d/1sFLG9mBvWYL4UZQ4eUQ3fqKqcXGJDut-ZxwwxJQV_w0/edit>`_,
and repeated below.

The Glasgow participants—and all members of the Transport Data Commons Initative—welcome
additional signatures from all who share the values and endorse the calls to action that it contains.

Full text of the Declaration
============================

WE—the undersigned individuals and organizations,
being experts and practitioners in transport development and data,
gathered in Glasgow on 24 February 2026—

RECOGNIZING that transport and mobility are vital to the wellbeing of all people,
and that accessible, high-quality data are essential
to evidence-based policymaking at local, national, and international levels,
serving to identify needs, set priorities, and guide investments
that leave no one behind;

NOTING that the UN General Assembly,
in declaring 2026–2035 the Decade of Sustainable Transport (the “Decade”),
called for systematic monitoring and data-driven tracking
to assess progress across the Decade’s six focus areas;

ACKNOWLEDGING that these efforts are central to advancing shared global agendas,
including the Sustainable Development Goals
and goals under the UN Framework Convention on Climate Change (UNFCCC);

CONCERNED that current transport data—often missing, fragmented, inaccessible
or inaccurate—are inadequate for our collective ambitions for sustainable development; 

FURTHER CONCERNED that these data gaps are most severe in low- and middle-income countries (LMICs),
where transport development is most urgently needed;

AWARE that these challenges arise from systemic barriers,
including limited institutional capacity,
insufficient funding for data infrastructure,
and misaligned incentives for data sharing;

CONVINCED that overcoming these barriers requires coordinated actions
across governments, international organizations, research institutions,
civil society, and the private sector;

DECLARE our shared vision of a global commons of transport data
in which all can contribute to developing high-quality data and metadata
as public goods, openly accessible for all to use.

We NOTE the achievements of the Transport Data Commons Initiative (TDCI) since 2022
in convening transport data users, providers, and practitioners worldwide,
and in establishing common practices, infrastructure and tools
grounded in open participation, established standards,
and the principles of Findable, Accessible, Interoperable and Reusable (FAIR) data;

And therefore we CALL UPON governments, international organizations,
development agencies, research institutions, civil society, and individuals
to take the specific actions outlined below,
in order to expand and sustain the Transport Data Commons
and thereby ensure that high-quality, accessible data
fully support the pursuit of shared goals, increased ambition,
and sustainable mobility for all.

Calls to action
---------------

We call on **governments** of all orders
(national, sub-national, and municipal)
and their units (departments, ministries, etc.) to:

- **Publish** openly, where feasible,
  transport data in machine-readable formats (e.g., .csv),
  recognizing that these data can be integrated in the TDC by the wider community.
- **Adopt** or **strengthen** policies
  that require transport data collected through public funding
  to be published openly and made accessible
  to researchers, planners, and the international community.
- **Nominate** TDC focal points within national transport ministries
  to coordinate with the TDC community on data sharing,
  ensure consistency of national datasets,
  and facilitate two-way knowledge exchange.

We call on **international development organizations (IDOs)**,
especially international finance institutions (IFIs)
such as multilateral development banks (MDBs),
bilateral development agencies, foundations, and philanthropies, to:

- **Mandate** that transport data generated through funded projects
  be uploaded to the Transport Data Commons in TDC-compatible formats,
  by including common data-sharing requirements
  in contracts with project consultants and implementing partners.
- **Strengthen capacity** in national and sub-national governments,
  in order to institutionalize regular collection and sharing of transport data
  in TDC-standard formats.
- **Join as TDC Supporters** in a tiered scheme of long-term financial support,
  in order to ensure TDC infrastructure and tools are well-maintained
  and developed according to needs of the global user community,
  and to fund ongoing work on standards development and harmonization.
- **Support** hackathons and similar events,
  for instance hosted at universities in LMICs,
  to support students and local users in expanding data literacy
  through TDC engagement.

We call on **knowledge organizations**
(universities, think tanks, and research institutions) both global and local to:

- **Contribute** transport data and metadata,
  including transport research outputs,
  to the TDC based on their local knowledge and expertise.
- **Partner** with LMIC institutions and organizations
  to build local capacity in transport data collection,
  analysis and sharing—including through collaborative research projects,
  workshops, and integration of TDC methods—
  to strengthen data ecosystems where they are most needed.

We call on **existing transport data initiatives**
to recognize the TDC as complementary infrastructure
that can amplify their visibility and impact,
and to coordinate with the TDC through dialogue and,
where appropriate, technical interoperability agreements,
in order to create a coherent data landscape that serves all users.

We call on **all stakeholders in the Transport Data Commons** to:

- **Develop** a governance structure for the TDCI
  that is inclusive with respect to geography, language, gender, race, etc.,
  and across categories of actors in transport data,
  including providers, users, and supporters,
  in order to ensure these diverse perspectives govern the future evolution of the TDC.
- **Contribute** to communities of practice convened by TDCI,
  including on the topic of ethics and justice in data practice.
- **Establish** regional working groups
  (e.g., TDC Africa, TDC Latin America)
  with TDCI facilitation,
  to coordinate data priorities,
  share best practices,
  and address context-specific challenges in data provision and use.

We call on **UN DESA and the UN regional commissions**,
as co-leads of the Decade, to:

- **Use** the TDC as a repository for transport data and metadata
  that underlies critical products,
  such as annual reports and dashboard(s),
  produced for comprehensive monitoring and tracking of progress
  within the Decade and its six focus areas.

We call on the **UN Economic and Social Council (ECOSOC)** to:

- **Receive and provide feedback** on annual TDCI progress reports
  at the High-Level Political Forum on Sustainable Development (HLPF-SD),
  offering guidance to strengthen implementation of these calls to action;
- **Invite** additional signatories,
  via new or strengthened calls and commitments,
  to raise the ambitions of the Declaration.

Finally, we DECLARE our own commitment to advocate for these calls
within our organizations and networks,
to contribute data and expertise to the TDC according to our respective capacities,
to participate actively in TDC governance structures and communities of practice,
and to lead by example in establishing the open, collaborative practices
that will sustain the commons for the benefit of all.
