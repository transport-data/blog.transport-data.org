A Charter for the Transport Data Commons Initiative
***************************************************

:slug: charter

As part of the `Glasgow Declaration <{filename}/2026-03-05-glasgow-declaration.rst>`_,
participants in the February 2026 TDC Global Launch event called on:

   …*all stakeholders in the Transport Data Commons* to [...]
   *Develop* a governance structure for the TDCI
   that is inclusive with respect to geography, language, gender, race, etc.,
   and across categories of actors in transport data,
   including providers, users, and supporters,
   in order to ensure these diverse perspectives govern the future evolution of the TDC.

In order to meet this call with action,
members of the TDCI founding group have worked during the past months to develop
a draft **Charter of the Transport Data Commons Initiative** (TDCI).

Read it `here <https://docs.google.com/document/d/1iYR3i3a7QZRdWWowTcmlKwg-UMR3wF78NiBZwJuEWDQ/edit>`_
—then read on to find out more about this document and how to help shape it.

Frequently asked questions (FAQs)
=================================

Why is a Charter needed?
   In the start-up phase of the TDCI,
   stakeholders and participants acted in an *ad-hoc* or
   “birds of a feather” manner—brought together by shared interest
   in the TDCI vision.

   After launching in 2026,
   the TDCI enters a new phase where *steady flows* of resources
   (people's time and money) are needed to support *continuous and sustained* work
   on the TDC infrastructure, tools, standards, community processes, (meta)data, etc.
   In order to collect and use these resources responsibly and effectively,
   the TDCI needs to *institutionalize*:
   to become an organization with clear,
   well-defined structure,
   duties, responsibilities, and procedures.

Why is the Charter so long/why are there so many different bodies and units?
   The TDCI itself brings together many types of actors,
   each of whom must do different, complementary things in order to make the TDC a success.

   During the 4+-year start-up phase of TDCI (from 2022 through 2026),
   those involved have talked to hundreds of stakeholders in transport data
   from around the world.
   After many hours of discussions,
   the structure in the Charter has evolved as the best way to handle two facts:

   1. Stakeholders and their organizations are extremely diverse,
      and have diverse roles and interests; and
   2. There are many different kinds of contributions (Appendix A1)
      that need to come together to achieve the TDCI mission.
   
   The Charter must provide a description of these people and groups;
   how they interact; and who is responsible to whom in what way.
   So the text is written to:

   - Give the simplest, clearest explanation of each of these bodies,
     how they relate,
     and essential TDCI activities.
   - Be specific and unambiguous,
     to avoid confusion or disputes about how it is interpreted.
   - At the same time, be *general, inclusive, and permissive*,
     avoid trying to limit the decision-making power of people
     who will fill the roles and bodies.

How is the Charter structured/organized?
  - Sections are all numbered.
    References to sections use the section mark: §.
    There is no hierarchy of sections.
  - Paragraphs and subparagraphs (“items”) are all numbered like 1.1(a)(i).
    References to items use the paragraph mark: ¶.
  - Key terms are underlined on first use.
  - Abbreviations written in full on first use (“United Nations (UN)”).
  - We avoid repeating “TDC” or “TDCI” except:
    (a) at the start of a section or
    (b) to avoid ambiguity.
  - We use present simple tense (“X does Y”) unless otherwise necessary.
